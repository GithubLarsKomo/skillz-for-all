# Authoritative source routing – German employment/labor law

Discovery baseline verified 2026-08-28. Runtime re-check via `current-law-context` is mandatory.

## Core official sources

- BGB, especially § 611a for employment-contract status and the actually applicable contract provisions.
- Kündigungsschutzgesetz (KSchG), including applicability, § 1 grounds and § 4 litigation deadline as triggered.
- Teilzeit- und Befristungsgesetz (TzBfG), including § 14 and applicable form/tariff overlays.
- Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz (AGG), especially scope, §§ 1, 7 and remedies/deadlines such as § 15 as triggered.
- Betriebsverfassungsgesetz (BetrVG), including participation/co-determination provisions; § 87(1) no. 6 only for an actually triggered technical-monitoring issue.
- Bundesdatenschutzgesetz (BDSG) § 26 and GDPR Article 88 for employee-data contexts, plus the otherwise applicable GDPR provisions.
- NachwG, ArbZG, BUrlG, EFZG and other specialist statutes only when facts trigger them.
- Tarifverträge, Betriebsvereinbarungen, individual contract terms and current BAG/other competent-court jurisprudence where material.

## Routing notes

- Special dismissal protection may require separate statutes/authority approvals; never infer completeness from KSchG alone.
- Social-security and tax classification can diverge from labor-law classification and should be separately routed.
- Cross-border employment needs conflict-of-law, mandatory-law and local specialist routing.
