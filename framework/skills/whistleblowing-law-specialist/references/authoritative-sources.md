# Authoritative source routing – whistleblowing / Germany / EU

Discovery baseline verified on 2026-08-28. **Re-check current versions at runtime via `current-law-context`; this file is not a substitute for current-law research.**

## Germany

- Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz (HinSchG), Gesetze im Internet. On 2026-08-28 the consolidated citation states last amendment by Article 2 of the Act of 22 July 2026 (BGBl. 2026 I No. 223).
  - § 8 confidentiality
  - § 10 processing of personal data by reporting offices
  - § 11 documentation and deletion rule
  - § 12 duty to establish internal reporting offices
  - §§ 13–18 tasks, organization, independence/expertise, channels, procedure and follow-up
  - §§ 33–39 protection requirements, retaliation, damages and non-waiver
  - § 40 administrative offences
- Bundesdatenschutzgesetz (BDSG), especially § 26 employee data and § 22 where special-category processing is relevant.
- Betriebsverfassungsgesetz (BetrVG), including § 87(1) no. 6 only where technical monitoring triggers co-determination.

## European Union

- Directive (EU) 2019/1937 on the protection of persons who report breaches of Union law; use the current consolidated EUR-Lex version.
- Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), in particular Articles 5, 6, 9, 10, 12–14, 32, 35 and 88 as triggered by the facts.

## Routing notes

- Sector-specific reporting systems may prevail or supplement HinSchG for their scope; verify the current special rule.
- Cross-border group hotlines require country-by-country legal routing rather than assuming German implementation governs all entities.
- Criminal-law, litigation, employment-sanction and privilege questions must be narrowed and routed through qualified specialists/counsel where needed.
